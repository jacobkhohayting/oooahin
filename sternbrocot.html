Stern Brocot Tree

Consider the function $f: \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}^{+}$ defined inductively by \[ f(1) = \dfrac{1}{1} \quad \text{and} \quad f(n+1) = \dfrac{1}{\lfloor f(n)\rfloor +1 - \{f(n)\}} \quad(n \geq 1).\] Here $\lfloor n \rfloor$ is the greatest integer in $n$ and $\{n\}$ is the fractional part of $n$.

We can model this function like a tree, as well.

Consider the infinite binary tree $T$ with root $\tfrac{1}{1}$. From each node $\tfrac{p}{q}$, the left child is $\tfrac{p+q}{q}$ while the right child is $\tfrac{p}{q+p}$.

To one's surprise, the values of $f$ and $T$ are indeed identical.
